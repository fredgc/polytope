# Polytope

The word "polytope" means the generalization of a polygon or polyhedron in
multiple dimensions. We can talk about the "cells" of a polytope, which are
the generalization of sides or faces.

This program only looks at regular convex polytopes. That means all of the
cells and angles are the same. In 3 dimensions, the five regular polytopes are
called the Platonic Solids.

## Types

### Simplex

A simplex is the smallest polytope. A line segment, a triangle, and a
tetrahedron are simplexes. In higher dimensions, an n-simplex is created by
taking an (n-1)-simplex and adding a new vertex above it connected to all
existing vertices. There is a simplex in every dimension.

### Hypercube

A hypercube (or n-cube) is the generalization of a square or cube. There is a
hypercube in every dimension.

### Cross Type (Orthoplex)

An n-dimensional cross-polytope is made by taking an (n-1)-dimensional cube
and adding one point above and one point below, connecting each vertex to both
new points. There is a cross-polytope in every dimension.

### Dodecahedron

The dodecahedron exists only in 3 dimensions and has 12 pentagonal faces.

### Icosahedron

The icosahedron exists only in 3 dimensions and has 20 triangular faces.

### 24-Cell

The 24-cell exists only in 4 dimensions and has 24 octahedral cells.
